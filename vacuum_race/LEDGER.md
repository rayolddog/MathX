| L25 | 2026-09-14 | A vacuum term that enters by interference with the particle's amplitude, so that its random part is linear in \|ψ\| as the cross term 2\|ψ_p\|\|ψ_v\| cos(φ_p − φ_v) is, selects in proportion to \|ψ\| and gives √r/(√r + 1) (0.667 at r = 4, 0.586 at r = 2): the square root of Born. Gaussian and bounded vacuum terms, multiplicative or additive, give the brighter pixel every time once the aperture holds a hundred atoms, and additive ones read the intensity difference rather than the ratio. | L26 | 2026-09-14 | The vacuum term that gives Born (L24) must be redrawn between arrivals. Frozen per atom it gives each detector an all-or-nothing split (or a share spread over [0, 1] with the phase mechanism, half the detectors at the extremes) and Born only across an ensemble of detectors; refreshed every τ arrivals it gives a Fano factor F = τ (about τ/2 with the phase weight) for one detector's split. Experiment: every SPAD pair gives the Born split within one detector with F = 1 at every counting rate, so τ is below one arrival interval (under about 10 ns at the fastest rates). Any vacuum timescale (10 to 100 fs in silicon) satisfies this, so the model passes, but only in the reading where the atom's vacuum term is a fluctuation faster than the arrivals, not a dressing fixed in advance; JB's "vacuum acts on the fermion first" holds only in that reading. Quantum mechanics has the same content as the memorylessness of the golden-rule rate (the Markov property of the coupling to a continuum of vacuum modes), which is where the exponential clock comes from. | addendum D8 to D12, P9 to P11, Tables 6 and 7, C5, C6 |
