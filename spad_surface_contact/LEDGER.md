| L13 | 2026-09-08 | Part D: with the bulk's second-spinor phase read as a driven transition coherence, the model predicts a pixel share that follows the particle's offset relative to the pulse phase (1.000 against Born's 0.800 for antiphase pixels) and an offset-averaged efficiency of 0.36 against 0.67, both decaying to the Born values within about one T2 of the preparation (10 to 100 fs in silicon at room temperature). | L14 | 2026-09-08 | Correction to L13, from its test (`L13_TEST.md`). Quantum mechanics does predict a phase-dependent response of a coherently prepared absorber to *coherent* light (coherent control; an antiphase second pulse destroys the excitons the first created, Heberle, Baumberg and Köhler 1995); the clause in L13 holds only for single quanta in Fock states. Against that, the model's deposit rule ½[1 + R cos Δφ] is never negative (no net stimulated emission), second order in the probe field where the measured fringe is first order (fringe/average ∝ 1/θ₂ for a weak probe against the model's constant R), and carries no population factor (quantum mechanics: absorption ∝ ρ_gg − ρ_ee, zero at inversion ½; the model: ½ of the ordinary deposit). Existing coherent-control data decide against the rule as written. The model's distinctive single-quantum prediction requires a definite offset δ_p per quantum, which no source controls and which the model has not defined for a photon. | Part D, A1, L13 |
| L15 | 2026-09-08 | Part E: a Dirac electron bound in a well has a second spinor slaved to the first, χ = −iφ′/(E_b + m − V), in quadrature (±90°) with it, fixed to 10⁻⁸ degrees and identical for every identical atom; its negative-energy free content is 10⁻⁶ of the norm at a binding of 0.03 rest energies and about 10⁻¹⁶ for a silicon valence electron, with no single offset and no counter-rotation (both free components rotate together at E_b).
